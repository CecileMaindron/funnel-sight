Spreadsheets vs a dedicated analytics tool for PLG tracking

A spreadsheet is usually the first place PLG funnel tracking lives. It's the right call for a while, and then it isn't. Here's where that switch usually happens, and what changes on the other side of it.

Where spreadsheets start to break

A spreadsheet is often the first tool a growth team reaches for. It's free, everyone already knows how to use it, and for a handful of signups it works fine. Then the account list grows. The event log gets exported from three separate places, and every dashboard update means someone rebuilds a pivot table by hand.

Formulas break when a column moves. Definitions drift, too: one person's "activated user" is not quite the next person's. By the time a report reaches the leadership team, the numbers behind it have usually gone stale.

What changes with a dedicated tool

Funnelsight pulls product usage and CRM data into one funnel view, so the definition of activation is set once and applied the same way across every report. If you're still deciding what that definition should even include, tracking activation metrics for PLG teams walks through how to set it. If you're not ready for a dedicated tool yet, you can get a first version of this working with just a spreadsheet import and one activation event.

Tracking activation events directly, instead of reconstructing them from exports, removes the manual rebuild step entirely. Teams that switch off spreadsheets don't need a data analyst to keep the setup running. Dashboards update as new usage data comes in, not when someone finds a free morning to refresh a pivot table, the same activation and retention numbers surfacing automatically in Funnelsight's dashboard.

A concrete example

Say a marketing team tracks trial signups in one spreadsheet and product usage exports in another, joining the two by hand every Monday. With Funnelsight connected to the product and the CRM, that same team sees signups, activation, and pipeline stage in a single funnel, updated automatically. Instead of losing a morning to reconciling two files, they spend it looking at what the drop-off actually shows.
