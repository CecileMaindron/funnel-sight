Marketing and Sales Are Looking at Different Funnel Numbers

Marketing counts a lead as qualified the moment it hits a score threshold. Sales counts it once they've actually talked to someone. Multiply that gap across every stage of the funnel, and by the time the two teams sit down for a pipeline review, they're arguing about whose spreadsheet is right instead of what to do next.

Why the numbers never match

Every tool has its own definition of a stage. The CRM says "opportunity created." The marketing platform says "lead converted." Someone built a dashboard three months ago, and nobody's touched the formulas since. None of this is anyone's fault, really. It's just what happens when funnel data lives in separate systems, refreshed on separate schedules, owned by separate teams.

The result is a familiar ritual: two decks, two sets of numbers, and the first fifteen minutes of every joint meeting spent reconciling them instead of discussing what to do about what they show.

One funnel, one definition

Funnelsight pulls funnel data into a single view that both teams look at, not two versions of the truth exported into separate slides. Stage definitions are set once and applied consistently across the whole pipeline, so everyone in the room is working from the same numbers before the conversation even starts.

When someone says "we're down 12% on stage two this month," there's only one 12% to argue about, and it's the actual problem, not the measurement.

What this looks like in practice

A growth marketer opens Funnelsight before a Monday pipeline review. The sales ops lead opens it an hour later, from a different laptop. Same funnel. Same stage counts. Same trend line. Neither one has exported anything to a spreadsheet first. If a number does shift between the two conversations, that's a signal something changed upstream, not something to debate in the meeting.
